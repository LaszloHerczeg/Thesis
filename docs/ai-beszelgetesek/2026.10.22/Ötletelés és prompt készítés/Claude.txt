Q:
I have a list of 13000 words extractred from legal documents. How can I decide whether a word's meaning is related to a divorce case or not?

A:
The most reliable approach combines evidence from your own documents with a semantic check, then validates the result on a small hand-labeled sample. No single method works well alone. Here's how I'd approach it.

**1. Decide what "related" means first.** Words fall on a spectrum. Some are core divorce terms (alimony, custody, dissolution). Some are family-law adjacent (prenuptial, paternity, guardianship). Some are general legal terms that appear everywhere (petition, hearing, plaintiff). The rest are unrelated. A 3–4 level scheme like this is easier to apply consistently than yes/no, and you can collapse it later.

**2. Use your documents if you can (strongest signal).** If you know which source documents are divorce cases and which aren't, run a keyness analysis. For each word, compare how often it appears in divorce documents with how often it appears in the others, using log-likelihood, a log-odds ratio, or TF-IDF per class. Words that are strongly overrepresented in divorce documents are your candidates. This is cheap and data-driven, and it captures your corpus's actual vocabulary, including local terms and jargon.

**3. Add a semantic similarity score.** Pick a small set of seed words: divorce, spouse, marriage, custody, alimony, child support, marital property. Embed every word with a legal or general embedding model, such as Legal-BERT, a sentence-transformer, or word2vec trained on your own documents. Score each word by its cosine similarity to the seeds. This catches related words that are rare in your corpus.

**4. Optionally, classify with an LLM.** 13,000 words is small enough to send in batches of a few hundred with a clear rubric (your categories from step 1) and ask for a label plus a confidence score. It's fast and often quite accurate, but treat it as one opinion, not ground truth.

**5. Watch out for ambiguous words.** Many words only belong to divorce in some contexts: custody (criminal vs. child), support, maintenance, settlement, separation, property, visitation. A word list strips away that context. For these, look at a few example sentences from your documents, or classify the word in context rather than in isolation.

**6. Validate.** Hand-label a random sample of 300–500 words, ideally with two people, and check their agreement using Cohen's kappa. Then measure how well your automatic method matches. This tells you where to set thresholds and whether the approach is trustworthy.

**A practical pipeline:**
1. Remove stopwords and general vocabulary. This often shrinks the list a lot.
2. Rank the rest by keyness and embedding similarity.
3. Auto-accept the clear top, auto-reject the clear bottom.
4. Manually review the uncertain middle band, plus the ambiguous words.

If the documents aren't in English, use embeddings for that language. Controlled vocabularies such as the EU's multilingual EuroVoc thesaurus, which has family-law descriptors, can also help as a reference list.

A:
That pattern usually has two separate causes, so it needs two separate filters.

**Why general legal terms show up:** they're overrepresented because your reference corpus is general language, or a mix that doesn't include enough legal text. Against everyday language, *every* legal word looks special.

**Fix: compare divorce documents against other legal documents.** Use court decisions from other case types (contracts, inheritance, criminal, labor, etc.) as the reference corpus. Words like *plaintiff*, *appeal*, *pursuant* are equally common in both, so they drop out. Only words specific to divorce stay overrepresented. If you don't have such documents, public court-decision databases in your language are a good source. The documents don't need to be labeled in detail, just "not divorce."

You can even run a two-stage comparison and keep the words that pass both:
1. legal vs. general language → identifies legal vocabulary
2. divorce vs. other legal → identifies divorce-specific vocabulary

Look at effect size (log ratio), not just significance. With large corpora, log-likelihood flags tiny differences as "significant." Require something like a log ratio ≥ 1 (at least twice as frequent) in addition to significance.

**Why non-legal words show up:** they're usually concentrated in a few documents: party names, towns, street names, a specific car or company in one case, dates, amounts, OCR errors, or boilerplate from one court's template. Their total count is high, but they aren't spread across cases.

**Fix: require dispersion, not just frequency.**
- Count in how many divorce documents each word appears (document frequency), and drop words that appear in fewer than, say, 5% of documents or fewer than 5–10 documents. Pick the threshold by checking a sample.
- Better still, run keyness on document frequency instead of raw counts, or use a dispersion measure (Gries' DP, Juilland's D).
- Remove names, places, numbers and dates with a named-entity recognizer and simple regex filters.

**For what's left: a semantic check.** Some general words will still slip through (e.g., *child*, *home*, *money*), which are related in context but not legal terms. Handle these with one of the following:
- Embeddings: compute each word's similarity to a set of divorce seed words *and* to a set of general legal seed words, and keep it only if it's closer to the divorce side.
- An LLM: classify the remaining words into 4 categories (divorce-specific / family-law / general legal / non-legal) with a short rubric. By this point the list should be small enough for a manual check of the uncertain cases.

**Combined filter:**

| Step | Removes |
|---|---|
| Divorce vs. other legal corpus, log ratio ≥ 1 | General legal terms |
| Document frequency ≥ threshold | Case-specific words, names, noise |
| NER + regex | Names, places, numbers, dates |
| Embedding or LLM category check | Remaining everyday words |
| Manual review of the borderline band | Errors |

Q:
What is log ratio?
I'm thinking that I could compare 2 different fields' list. Like worklaw and divorce. If a word is in both category, they are probably not related to a specific field

A:
**Log ratio** measures how many times more frequent a word is in one corpus than in another, on a log scale.

First you normalize the counts, because your two corpora probably aren't the same size. Then you take the base-2 logarithm of the ratio:

> log ratio = log₂ ( (count in divorce ÷ total words in divorce) / (count in labor law ÷ total words in labor law) )

How to read it:

| Log ratio | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Equally frequent in both |
| 1 | 2× more frequent in divorce |
| 2 | 4× more frequent |
| 3 | 8× more frequent |
| −1, −2 … | More frequent in labor law |

An example, assuming both corpora have 1 million words:

| Word | Divorce | Labor law | Log ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| alimony | 300 | 3 | +6.6 → divorce-specific |
| court | 2000 | 1900 | +0.07 → general legal |
| employee | 50 | 4000 | −6.3 → labor-specific |

If a word never appears in one corpus, the ratio is undefined. The usual workaround is to add a small number (e.g. 0.5) to every count before dividing.

**About your idea:** comparing two fields is exactly the right direction. That's the "divorce vs. other legal documents" comparison. But I'd compare **frequencies**, not just whether a word is present in both lists. Presence is too blunt:

- With thousands of documents, almost every common word shows up in both fields at least once. *Alimony* will probably appear somewhere in labor law too, for example when wages are garnished for support. A strict "in both → remove" rule would throw away many words you want to keep.
- Frequency handles this naturally: 300 vs. 3 is clearly divorce, while 2000 vs. 1900 is clearly shared. The log ratio is just a way to put a number on that difference.

**One more suggestion: use 3 or more fields if you can.** Divorce and labor law share some topics that other fields don't, such as income, salary and employers (relevant to support payments). With only labor law as the comparison, those words look "general" when they aren't. If you add, say, inheritance, contract and criminal cases, then:

- General legal terms are frequent in **all** fields → low log ratio against every one → remove
- Divorce terms are frequent only in divorce → high log ratio against all others → keep

A simple rule is to keep a word if its log ratio is ≥ 1 against *each* comparison field, or against all of them combined into one reference corpus.

When the top of the list is dominated by unrelated words, the problem is usually the type of counting, not the amount of data. A party's name repeated 80 times in one case then counts as 1, while *custody* appearing in 6 of your 8 divorce cases counts as 6. This alone often clears most of the noise. Also lemmatize, so *divorced*, *divorcing* and *divorce* pool into one count, and strip names and numbers.

- keep a word only if it also appears in the statute/glossary, or
- use those texts as your "divorce" corpus while your case collection is still small.

The same works for labor law: take the labor code as the comparison text.

Q:
what are n-grams?

A:
An n-gram is a sequence of *n* consecutive words treated as one unit.

- **1-gram (unigram):** a single word, e.g. *szülői*, *felügyelet*
- **2-gram (bigram):** two words in a row, e.g. *szülői felügyelet*
- **3-gram (trigram):** three words in a row, e.g. *közös szülői felügyelet*

Your list already contains some. *elsőfokú bíróság*, *szülői felügyelet*, *folyamatos kapcsolattartás* and *fővárosi törvényszék* are bigrams, which is why they appear next to their single-word parts.

They matter because some meanings only exist in the combination. *közös* alone is an everyday word, and *érdek* alone is too, but *közös szülői felügyelet* and *gyermek érdeke* are clearly divorce terms. Similarly, *páros* is general, but *páros hét* is a visitation schedule. If you only count single words, you lose that information.

How they're made: slide a window over the text. From "a gyermek érdeke szerint", the bigrams are *a gyermek*, *gyermek érdeke*, *érdeke szerint*. Most of these combinations are meaningless, so you usually keep only bigrams that occur often enough, and the log ratio comparison then filters them the same way as single words.

That's why step 2 said to process both sets with the same n-gram settings: if you create bigrams for the divorce decisions, create them for the reference decisions too, so you can compare their counts.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
jogi_kulcsszavak.py - characteristic lemmas / n-grams of a legal field.

Compares a TARGET folder of Hungarian case descriptions (.txt, one case per
file, UTF-8) with a REFERENCE folder of general cases and finds 1-, 2- and
3-word lemma sequences that are over-represented in the target field.

Two sub-commands:

  extract  - lemmatizes both folders with HuSpaCy (hu_core_news_lg) and writes
             the full table (step 1). This is the slow part.
  filter   - reads the step-1 CSV and keeps the lemmas that pass the
             thresholds (step 2). No text processing, runs in seconds.

Examples:
  python jogi_kulcsszavak.py extract csaladjog/ altalanos/ -o step1_teljes.csv
  python jogi_kulcsszavak.py filter step1_teljes.csv -o step2_szurt.csv
  python jogi_kulcsszavak.py filter step1_teljes.csv -o step2_szurt.csv --min-docs 5 --min-log-ratio 1.5
"""

import argparse
import csv
import io
import math
import re
import sys
import time
from collections import Counter
from pathlib import Path

MODEL_NAME = "hu_core_news_lg"
MAX_N = 3

# Tokens with these POS tags end the current run of words, so no n-gram can
# contain or span across them (punctuation, numerals, symbols).
BREAK_POS = {"PUNCT", "NUM", "SYM"}

# A valid lemma is made of letters only, optionally joined by a hyphen or an
# apostrophe (e.g. "ptk", "szülői", "kft", "e-mail"). [^\W\d_] = any Unicode
# letter, so á, é, ő, ű etc. are all included.
WORD_RE = re.compile(r"[^\W\d_]+(?:[-'’][^\W\d_]+)*")
DIGIT_RE = re.compile(r"\d")
WHITESPACE_RE = re.compile(r"\s+")

STEP1_COLUMNS = ["lemma", "n", "d", "N_d", "r", "N_r", "log_ratio", "total_d"]
STEP2_COLUMNS = ["lemma", "total_d"]


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Reading files
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def read_folder(folder: Path, label: str):
    """Return [(file name, normalized text)] for every .txt file in the folder."""
    if not folder.is_dir():
        sys.exit(f"ERROR: the {label} folder does not exist: {folder}")
    files = sorted(p for p in folder.iterdir()
                   if p.is_file() and p.suffix.lower() == ".txt")
    if not files:
        sys.exit(f"ERROR: no .txt files found in the {label} folder: {folder}")

    docs = []
    for path in files:
        raw = path.read_bytes()
        try:
            text = raw.decode("utf-8-sig")          # also strips a BOM if present
        except UnicodeDecodeError:
            print(f"  WARNING: {path.name} is not valid UTF-8; "
                  f"undecodable bytes were replaced.", file=sys.stderr)
            text = raw.decode("utf-8-sig", errors="replace")
        # Line breaks (and any other whitespace run) become a single space,
        # so an n-gram can continue on the next line.
        text = WHITESPACE_RE.sub(" ", text).strip()
        if not text:
            print(f"  WARNING: {path.name} is empty (still counted as a document).",
                  file=sys.stderr)
        docs.append((path.name, text))
    return docs


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# NLP
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def load_model():
    try:
        import spacy
    except ImportError:
        sys.exit("ERROR: spaCy is not installed. Run: pip install huspacy")
    try:
        nlp = spacy.load(MODEL_NAME)
    except OSError:
        sys.exit(f"ERROR: the {MODEL_NAME} model is not installed.\n"
                 f'Run: python -c "import huspacy; huspacy.download(\'{MODEL_NAME}\')"')

    # Named entity recognition is not needed; switching it off saves time.
    unneeded = [name for name in ("ner",) if name in nlp.pipe_names]
    if unneeded:
        nlp.select_pipes(disable=unneeded)

    # Make sure sentence boundaries are available; add a rule-based
    # sentencizer only if the model itself does not set them.
    probe = nlp("Ez az első mondat. Ez a második mondat.")
    if not probe.has_annotation("SENT_START"):
        nlp.add_pipe("sentencizer", first=True)

    from spacy.lang.hu.stop_words import STOP_WORDS
    return nlp, frozenset(w.lower() for w in STOP_WORDS)


def clean_lemma(tok):
    """Lowercased lemma of a word token, or None if the token breaks n-grams."""
    if tok.is_punct or tok.pos_ in BREAK_POS or DIGIT_RE.search(tok.text):
        return None
    lemma = (tok.lemma_ or tok.text).strip().lower()
    lemma = lemma.strip(".")                 # abbreviations: "Ptk." -> "ptk"
    if not WORD_RE.fullmatch(lemma):
        return None
    return lemma


def word_runs(doc):
    """Yield lists of consecutive word lemmas. A run never crosses a sentence
    boundary, punctuation, a number or a symbol."""
    for sent in doc.sents:
        run = []
        for tok in sent:
            if tok.is_space:
                continue
            lemma = clean_lemma(tok)
            if lemma is None:
                if run:
                    yield run
                run = []
            else:
                run.append(lemma)
        if run:
            yield run


def ngrams_of_runs(runs, stopwords, max_n=MAX_N):
    """Count 1..max_n-grams in the runs. N-grams starting or ending with a
    stopword are dropped; stopwords inside a 3-gram are kept."""
    counts = Counter()
    for run in runs:
        length = len(run)
        for n in range(1, max_n + 1):
            for i in range(length - n + 1):
                if run[i] in stopwords or run[i + n - 1] in stopwords:
                    continue
                counts[" ".join(run[i:i + n])] += 1
    return counts


def analyze(nlp, docs, stopwords, label, batch_size, n_process):
    """Yield an n-gram Counter for each document, with progress output."""
    texts = [text for _, text in docs]
    longest = max((len(t) for t in texts), default=0)
    if longest >= nlp.max_length:
        nlp.max_length = longest + 1

    total = len(texts)
    step = max(1, total // 20)
    start = time.time()
    print(f"Lemmatizing {label} folder ({total} documents)...", file=sys.stderr)
    for i, doc in enumerate(nlp.pipe(texts, batch_size=batch_size,
                                     n_process=n_process), start=1):
        yield ngrams_of_runs(word_runs(doc), stopwords)
        if i % step == 0 or i == total:
            elapsed = time.time() - start
            print(f"  {label}: {i}/{total} documents ({elapsed:.0f} s)",
                  file=sys.stderr)

def write_csv(path: Path, header, rows, delimiter):
    # utf-8-sig writes a BOM, which makes Excel recognise UTF-8 so that
    # á, é, ő, ű etc. display correctly.
    with open(path, "w", encoding="utf-8-sig", newline="") as f:
        writer = csv.writer(f, delimiter=delimiter)
        writer.writerow(header)
        writer.writerows(rows)


def read_csv(path: Path):
    """Read a step-1 CSV written with either ',' or ';' as delimiter.
    Returns (rows as dicts, delimiter)."""
    if not path.is_file():
        sys.exit(f"ERROR: file not found: {path}")
    text = path.read_text(encoding="utf-8-sig")
    first_line = text.split("\n", 1)[0]
    delimiter = ";" if first_line.count(";") > first_line.count(",") else ","
    reader = csv.DictReader(io.StringIO(text, newline=""), delimiter=delimiter)
    missing = {"lemma", "d", "log_ratio", "total_d"} - set(reader.fieldnames or [])
    if missing:
        sys.exit(f"ERROR: {path} is not a step-1 table "
                 f"(missing columns: {', '.join(sorted(missing))}).")
    return list(reader), delimiter


def to_float(value: str) -> float:
    return float(value.strip().replace(",", "."))   # accepts decimal comma too


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Step 1: extract
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def cmd_extract(args):
    target_docs = read_folder(Path(args.target), "target")
    ref_docs = read_folder(Path(args.reference), "reference")
    N_d, N_r = len(target_docs), len(ref_docs)

    print(f"Loading {MODEL_NAME}...", file=sys.stderr)
    nlp, stopwords = load_model()

    doc_freq_target = Counter()   # d: number of target documents containing it
    total_target = Counter()      # total_d: occurrences in all target documents
    for counts in analyze(nlp, target_docs, stopwords, "target",
                          args.batch_size, args.n_process):
        total_target.update(counts)
        doc_freq_target.update(counts.keys())

    # For the reference folder only lemmas that occur in the target matter,
    # so only those are counted (keeps memory usage low).
    vocabulary = doc_freq_target.keys()
    doc_freq_ref = Counter()      # r: number of reference documents containing it
    for counts in analyze(nlp, ref_docs, stopwords, "reference",
                          args.batch_size, args.n_process):
        doc_freq_ref.update(k for k in counts if k in vocabulary)

    rows = []
    for lemma, d in doc_freq_target.items():
        r = doc_freq_ref.get(lemma, 0)
        log_ratio = math.log2(((d + 0.5) / (N_d + 1)) / ((r + 0.5) / (N_r + 1)))
        rows.append((lemma, lemma.count(" ") + 1, d, N_d, r, N_r,
                     round(log_ratio, 3), total_target[lemma]))

    # log_ratio descending; ties: more target documents first, then alphabetical
    rows.sort(key=lambda row: (-row[6], -row[2], row[0]))

    if args.excel_hu:
        delimiter = ";"
        out_rows = [row[:6] + (f"{row[6]:.3f}".replace(".", ","),) + row[7:]
                    for row in rows]
    else:
        delimiter = ","
        out_rows = [row[:6] + (f"{row[6]:.3f}",) + row[7:] for row in rows]

    out_path = Path(args.output)
    write_csv(out_path, STEP1_COLUMNS, out_rows, delimiter)

    by_n = Counter(row[1] for row in rows)
    print()
    print("=== Summary (step 1) ===")
    print(f"Target documents:     {N_d}")
    print(f"Reference documents:  {N_r}")
    print(f"Lemmas / n-grams:     {len(rows)}  "
          f"(1-word: {by_n[1]}, 2-word: {by_n[2]}, 3-word: {by_n[3]})")
    print(f"Written to:           {out_path}")


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Step 2: filter
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def cmd_filter(args):
    in_path = Path(args.input)
    rows, delimiter = read_csv(in_path)

    kept = []
    for row in rows:
        try:
            d = int(row["d"])
            log_ratio = to_float(row["log_ratio"])
            total_d = int(row["total_d"])
        except (TypeError, ValueError):
            print(f"  WARNING: skipping unreadable row: {row}", file=sys.stderr)
            continue
        if d >= args.min_docs and log_ratio >= args.min_log_ratio:
            kept.append((row["lemma"], total_d))

    kept.sort(key=lambda item: (-item[1], item[0]))   # total_d descending

    out_path = Path(args.output)
    write_csv(out_path, STEP2_COLUMNS, kept, delimiter)

    n_docs_info = ""
    if rows:
        n_docs_info = (f"Target documents:     {rows[0].get('N_d', '?')}\n"
                       f"Reference documents:  {rows[0].get('N_r', '?')}\n")
    print("=== Summary (step 2) ===")
    print(n_docs_info, end="")
    print(f"Thresholds:           d >= {args.min_docs}, "
          f"log_ratio >= {args.min_log_ratio}")
    print(f"Lemmas before filter: {len(rows)}")
    print(f"Lemmas after filter:  {len(kept)}")
    print(f"Written to:           {out_path}")

Q:
megvan az ambiguous_keywords dict-em
azon gondolkozok, hogy hogyan tegyek fel tisztázó kérdéseket
gondolom végigiterálok a tokeneken, felveszem listába az összes ambiguous keyword-öt, markolom a case-t mint more information needed és elmegyünk egy megfelelő viewba

A:
Az irány jó, három dolgot tennék hozzá.

**1. Előbb pontozz, csak utána kérdezz.** Ha a kategorizálás már így is egyértelmű (a második kategória az első 50%-a alatt van), akkor ne kérdezz, akkor sem, ha van kétértelmű szó a szövegben. A sorrend így néz ki:
- egyértelmű → kész, nincs kérdés
- nem egyértelmű, és van kétértelmű szó → „more information needed”, kérdések
- nem egyértelmű, és nincs kétértelmű szó → sikertelen kategorizálás oldal

**2. Hagyd ki a már tisztázott szavakat.** Ha az ügyfél a *szerződés* mellett azt is leírta, hogy *albérletszerződés* vagy *munkaszerződés*, akkor a *szerződés*-re nem kell rákérdezni. Tehát egy kétértelmű kulcsszó csak akkor kerül a listára, ha egyik hozzá tartozó hosszabb kifejezés sincs a szövegben.

**3. Ne csak tokeneken iterálj.** Mivel a kétértelmű kulcsok között n=2 kifejezések is vannak, a szöveget ugyanúgy kell feldolgoznod, mint a lista készítésekor: ugyanazzal a lemmatizálással és ugyanazzal az n-gram függvénnyel (1–3). Különben a *fizetem a tartást* sosem fog egyezni a *tartás* lemmával, és a kétszavas kulcsok kimaradnak.

**A view-ban:**
- Kulcsszavanként egy kérdés, az opciók a hozzá tartozó hosszabb kifejezések, plusz egy „egyik sem”.
- Legfeljebb 2–3 kérdés legyen, a legnagyobb hatásúak előre (pl. a legnagyobb súlyú opciók alapján). Ha túl sokat kérdezel, az ügyfél feladja.
- A válasz után a választott kifejezés pontjait hozzáadod a megfelelő kategóriához, és **újraszámolod**. Ha még mindig nem egyértelmű, menjen a sikertelen oldalra. Csak egyszer kérdezz, különben végtelen kör lehet belőle.
- Az eredeti leírást és a válaszokat is mentsd el az ügyhöz, később ezekből tanulhatsz (pl. milyen szavakat használnak az ügyfelek).

most itt tartok

A:
A lényeg, hogy ne tokenenként menj végig, hanem ugyanazokon az 1–3 szavas n-gramokon, amikből a listák készültek. Erre a legbiztosabb megoldás, hogy a listakészítő szkript függvényeit használod, így a feldolgozás garantáltan ugyanaz:

```python
from jogi_kulcsszavak import load_model, word_runs, ngrams_of_runs

# Egyszer töltsd be, modulszinten, ne minden classify hívásnál
# (a modell betöltése több másodperc és sok memória).
NLP, STOPWORDS = load_model()
HAZASSAG_DICT, MUNKAVISZONY_DICT = normalize_lemmas()
AMBIGUOUS = create_ambiguous_keyword_dictionary()


def extract_ngrams(text):
    text = " ".join(text.split())          # sortörések -> szóköz, mint a listáknál
    doc = NLP(text)
    return ngrams_of_runs(word_runs(doc), STOPWORDS)   # Counter: {"szülői felügyelet": 2, ...}


def classify(case, mode):
    ngrams = extract_ngrams(case.description)

    hazassag_point = 0
    munkaviszony_point = 0
    for ngram, count in ngrams.items():
        if ngram in HAZASSAG_DICT:
            hazassag_point += HAZASSAG_DICT[ngram] * count
        if ngram in MUNKAVISZONY_DICT:
            munkaviszony_point += MUNKAVISZONY_DICT[ngram] * count

    high = max(hazassag_point, munkaviszony_point)
    low = min(hazassag_point, munkaviszony_point)
    clear = high >= 6000 and low * 2 <= high

    if not clear:
        # kétértelmű kulcsszavak, amiknek egyik hosszabb formája sincs a szövegben
        unresolved = [
            kw for kw in ngrams
            if kw in AMBIGUOUS
            and not any(phrase in ngrams for phrase in AMBIGUOUS[kw])
        ]
        if unresolved:
            case.status = Case.Status.MORE_INFO_NEEDED
            case.save(update_fields=["status"])
            return unresolved          # ezekből építed a kérdéseket a view-ban
        case.status = Case.Status.NOT_SUPPORTED
        case.save(update_fields=["status"])
        return None

    name = "Családjog" if hazassag_point > munkaviszony_point else "Munkajog"
    category = Category.objects.get(name=name)
    case.category = category
    case.save()
    return Classification.objects.create(case=case, predicted_category=category, mode=mode)
```

Pár megjegyzés:

- **Hiba volt a régi feltételben:** ha a két pontszám egyenlő (pl. mindkettő 0), egyik ág sem kapta el, és az `else`-ben a `category[0]` egy tetszőleges kategóriát adott. A `high`/`low` változat ezt kezeli, és az 50%-os szabályt is egyszerűbben fejezi ki.
- A `load_model` a parsert is betölti, mert a mondathatárokhoz kell. A te `exclude=["parser", ...]` hívásoddal a `doc.sents` hibát dobna.
- A `jogi_kulcsszavak.py`-nak importálhatónak kell lennie, vagyis ugyanabban a mappában vagy source rootban legyen, mint ez a fájl.
- Azt feltételeztem, hogy az `AMBIGUOUS[kw]` a hosszabb kifejezések listája. Ha tuple-öket tárolsz (kifejezés, kategória, súly), akkor `phrase[0] in ngrams` kell.
- A `MORE_INFO_NEEDED` státuszt fel kell venned a `Case.Status`-ba, és az `unresolved` listát valahol el kell tárolnod (pl. JSONField vagy session), hogy a view megkapja.
- Todo-ra: most a *szülői felügyelet* kétszer is pontot ad, egyszer a 2-gramként, egyszer a *szülői* és a *felügyelet* szavakként. Mindkét kategóriánál hasonlóan torzít, de később érdemes lehet kezelni.
